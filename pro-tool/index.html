Champions Battle Data Tool

Team preview gives you about a minute to read six Pokemon you have never seen together. This tool does that part for you: both teams side by side, who resists what, who outspeeds whom, the moves you have to play around, and a lead that has been tested rather than picked on a feeling. With Pro it does all of it live, while the game is running.

Battle Mode

The matchup, scored in both directions

Your six and the opponent's six sit side by side with their stats, moves, items and abilities. The Matchup Overview between them counts, for every Pokemon on both sides, how many of the other team it resists, how many it is effective into, how many it outspeeds, and what its offensive and defensive pressure comes to. You read the good and bad matchups off a table instead of assembling them in your head.

Underneath it, Moves to watch out for lists the specific opponent moves that beat you, each with its usage percentage, so you know how likely the thing you are afraid of actually is.

Speed Ranking, Import Opponent Team, and the most common moves, items, abilities and spreads for anything on the board are all one click away in the same view.

Damage Calculator

Every field condition that decides the turn

Both Pokemon, both formats, and the field state that actually matters: weather, terrain, screens, Tailwind, Friend Guard, Trick Room, hazards, status and stat stages. It reports KO odds rather than a bare damage range.

Four variants of every matchup are precomputed (neither item active, attacker's item, defender's item, both), so toggling a Life Orb is instant, and any slot can be imported straight from a saved team instead of retyping a spread.

Team Builder

Build against the ladder, not against a type chart

A team is scored against the current top 30 of the ladder. You get average coverage and average offensive pressure per type, so a 0% column tells you exactly which type you cannot threaten, and which one is about to threaten you.

Alongside it sit a Synergy Overview, an Offense and Defense Pressure Overview, Speed Control for the Tailwind and Trick Room picture, Lead and Back planning, and a Battle Gameplan for the matchups you expect. Suggestions arrive with a Why this fits breakdown rather than a bare score, and the team-building checks themselves are yours to customise.

Teams are saved without limit, searched, tagged by archetype, duplicated, imported and exported.

Team Evaluation, in Pro

The verdict on a finished team, threat by threat

Team Evaluation scores a team on synergy, offense, defense and speed, and then shows its working. Every score opens into the overview behind it, so a low number tells you where it came from rather than only how bad it is.

The team-building checks run down one side: archetype detection, Mega options, speed control, Protect and positioning, spread damage, priority and cleanup, physical and special pressure, utility and disruption. Each one passes or fails with the reason attached and the Pokemon responsible named.

Critical Threats is the part that changes how you build. Every serious threat in the top 40 of the meta is listed with its real spread and usage, how much pressure it puts on you (how many of your six it OHKOs and 2HKOs), how many answers you actually have, and the single best answer you own with the damage roll behind it. A team either has an answer or it does not, and this tells you which.

Auto Build Team runs the same engine in reverse, and it is the most powerful thing in the tool: give it what you own and it assembles a team against the current meta, showing which threats each pick is there to answer as it goes.

Live Mode, in Pro

It watches the game and keeps up, so you do not have to

This is the part Pro exists for. The game goes into the tool as a video feed and Live Mode reads it as it happens. The opponent's team is recognised straight from team preview, so nothing has to be typed in, and the lead and back suggestions recalculate on their own as the picture changes.

You spend team preview reading a recommendation instead of racing the timer to enter six Pokemon, and the matchup, the speed order and the suggestion stay current for the rest of the game without you touching anything.

Getting the game into the tool

  • Android. Connect the phone by USB and the tool mirrors it. You can play the game inside the app itself, on the same screen as your data, with no extra hardware at all.
  • Switch. A capture card sits between the console and the PC. It does not need to be an expensive one: a basic HDMI capture stick from Amazon is around ten dollars and is all this needs.

Streaming your game into the tool and recording your matches are free. Live Mode is the part that reads the feed for you.

Top Lead, in Pro

A lead that has been played out thousands of times

Top Lead runs the opening through a Monte Carlo tree search across every legal four-Pokemon selection on both sides, and ranks the lead and back pairs that come out ahead. Each one carries a simulated win share, a lower-tail safety score for how badly the line can go wrong, and a confidence figure, so a fragile 70% and a reliable 70% do not look identical.

The run in this screenshot finished 1,200 simulations in 23.67 seconds. You can also let it run until you press stop, and open any result as an example rollout with the full turn log behind it.

The resolver is not a heuristic. All 954 moves and 200 abilities are compiled from their descriptions into 240 move operations and 133 ability operations, and the same resolver drives the damage calculator, so the two never disagree.

And the rest of it

Everything else in the box

  • Battle Data, offline. 236 ranked Pokemon, 227 species with their forms, 954 moves and 199 items, searchable without a connection in Doubles or Singles.
  • Record Games. Recording trims itself down to the game, so you keep the match and not the queue around it, and you can step back through it turn by turn afterwards.
  • Stream. Mirror your phone or a capture card so the game sits on the same screen as your data.
  • Box scanning, in Pro. Point it at your Pokemon Box and it reads your Pokemon in, so a team can be built from what you actually own rather than from what you are willing to type.

Where the money goes

Pro is what keeps the free side free

This project has a running cost that is easy to miss. A dedicated machine sits and collects ladder snapshots for all 236 ranked Pokemon every single day, in both formats, and that data is then cleaned, checked and published. The website, the daily meta trends and the public API are given away on top of that.

There is no advertising here and no data sold to anyone. Pro subscriptions are the only thing paying for it, which is also why the free tool is not deliberately limited: the site, the API and the free version stay exactly as they are whether or not you ever subscribe. If the tool earns a place in your routine, subscribing is the most direct way to keep the rest of it running.

Before you decide

Questions worth asking

Is the free version a trial?
No. It does not expire and it is not time-limited. Battle Mode with the full matchup overview, the damage calculator, the team builder, unlimited saved teams and the offline data are the free version permanently.
What do I need to get the game into the tool?
On Android, a USB cable. The tool mirrors the phone and you can play inside the app itself. On Switch you need a capture card between the console and the PC, and a basic HDMI capture stick at around ten dollars does the job.
Does this interact with the game in any way?
No. Streaming and Live Mode both read a video feed. The tool sends no input to the game, reads no game memory and modifies no game files. Everything runs locally.
What do I need to run it?
A Windows PC. Streaming, Live Mode and Box scanning additionally need a USB-connected Android phone or a capture card. Everything else works on the machine on its own.
Can I cancel?
Yes, at any time. The yearly plan is simply cheaper per month rather than a longer commitment, and cancelling returns you to the free version rather than locking you out of your teams.
Is the data the same as this website's?
Yes, the same daily dataset. The tool ships with it packaged so it works offline, and the website and API remain the free public view of it.
